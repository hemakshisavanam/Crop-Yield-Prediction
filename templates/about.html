About AgriSense

An AI-based agricultural decision-support system designed to forecast crop yield, analyze historical climate trends, and assist farmers with data-driven agronomic intelligence.

Project Objectives & Vision

Agricultural productivity is heavily influenced by dynamic environmental factors, including temperature shifts, erratic rainfall patterns, and pest management. AgriSense bridges the gap between historical agricultural records and predictive analytics, giving farmers and agronomists an intuitive tool to evaluate how environmental variations impact harvest yields.

Yield Estimation

Multivariable regression predicting harvest outputs in hg/ha, kg/ha, and tonnes/ha.

Smart AI Guidance

Google Gemini API integration paired with a robust agronomic fallback engine.

Multi-Crop Comparison

Benchmarking alternative staples under identical agro-climatic scenarios.

Future Simulations

Statistical model extrapolations for future growing seasons (2024–2035).

Dataset & Machine Learning Pipeline

AgriSense is trained exclusively on verified historical data sourced from the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO). All evaluation metrics are directly generated by training on an 80/20 train/test split.

Model Limitations & Scientific Honesty

AgriSense provides decision support, not absolute guarantees. The underlying machine-learning algorithm models historical patterns across regional aggregates and does not account for:

  • Real-time localized micro-weather (e.g. sudden flash floods, unseasonal hail, or frost snaps).
  • Soil chemistry and biology (e.g. precise soil pH, organic carbon content, macronutrient NPK depletion).
  • Seed variety genetics (e.g. hybrid vs. heirloom seeds, drought-tolerant or GM traits).
  • Biological outbreaks (e.g. novel locust swarms or sudden blight epidemics).
  • Socioeconomic variables (e.g. fertilizer market prices, labor availability, post-harvest storage losses).
Mandatory Disclaimer: Predictions generated by AgriSense are mathematical estimations intended for academic and decision-support purposes. They must not be treated as certified professional agronomic guarantees.
